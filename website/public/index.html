    Arranged the way you think.

    LaunchGrid finds the apps you have installed and lays them out by category or alphabetically. From there, the grid is yours.

    • Folders and pages

      Hold one app over another to make a folder. Drag to the edge of the screen to move to the next page, or onto a new one.

    • Search that guesses well

      Names that start with what you typed come first, then word starts, then initials, so vsc finds Visual Studio Code. Return opens the top result.

    • New apps find their place

      Install an app and it appears on the grid. Delete one and it disappears. Apps you removed by choice stay removed.

    • Full screen or a window

      Cover the screen, stop above the Dock, or use a resizable window. Choose a blurred desktop, your wallpaper, Liquid Glass on macOS 26, a solid colour or a picture of your own.

    • Undo anything

      Fifty levels of undo, including imports, rebuilds and changes to the grid size. Save your layout to a file and load it on another Mac.

    Made for the keyboard.

    Everything works with a mouse or trackpad too: swipe between pages, right-click for options.

    ⌥ Space Show or hide the grid. You can pick another shortcut in Settings.
    Type Search. Return opens the first result.
    Arrow keys Move between apps. Return opens one.
    ⌘ 1 to 9 Jump to a page. ← → and Tab step through them.
    ⌘ E Edit the layout: select, rename, group and remove.
    ⌘ G Put the selected apps in a folder.
    ⌘ Z Undo. ⇧ ⌘ Z redoes.
    Esc Clear the search, close the folder, leave edit mode, clear the selection, then hide.

    Nothing leaves your Mac.

    LaunchGrid makes no network connections. It has no account, no analytics, no ads and no purchases. Your layout and settings are stored on your Mac and nowhere else.

    The privacy policy says exactly what it reads and where it keeps it.

    Why it exists.

    The developer wanted a launcher that opens the moment you ask for it. LaunchGrid is a native Mac app written in Swift, with nothing between you and your apps but a grid and a search field.

    There's no paywall, because there's nothing to put behind one. LaunchGrid is a deliberately small app that was written in a single day, and charging for it wouldn't be fair.

    It's also practice. Sébastien wanted to learn what it takes to publish on the App Store, and a simple, finished app made a good first assignment.

    How it was made.

    About 99% of LaunchGrid was written by AI: its code, its tests, and the text and design of this website were generated by Claude, Anthropic's AI model. Sébastien decided what LaunchGrid should do, directed the work, tested it and chose what ships, and is responsible for it, bugs included.

    None of that AI is in the app. LaunchGrid contains no AI features and doesn't connect to any AI service, or to anything else. The source code is on GitHub, so you can read exactly what it does.
